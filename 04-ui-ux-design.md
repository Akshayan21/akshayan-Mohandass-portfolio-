# Work through the product decisions before they become development changes.

A user reaches a screen. What do they need to understand? What can they do? What happens if the information is missing, the request fails, or their role changes?

These are design decisions as much as technical ones.

I help startup founders turn product requirements into clear user journeys, interfaces, and clickable prototypes.

I’m Akshayan Mohandass, a freelance UI/UX designer working with teams across India.

## Start with the journey your product depends on.

Your first engagement doesn’t need to cover the entire application.

It can focus on the experience that matters most right now:

- Helping a new user reach their first useful action
- Completing a booking or request
- Finding and reviewing a record
- Understanding a dashboard and choosing the next task
- Moving work between different roles
- Recovering from an error or incomplete submission

We’ll define the boundaries of that journey before counting screens.

## UI/UX design grounded in how the product needs to work

### Turn requirements into a user flow.

A requirements document often mixes business rules, features, and implementation assumptions.

We’ll separate those concerns and map the sequence a user needs to follow. Permissions, dependencies, and unanswered questions become visible early.

### Establish the structure before polishing the screens.

Wireframes help us review information order, navigation, and actions without getting distracted by visual details.

You can assess whether the proposed experience makes sense before we develop its appearance.

### Design the interface and its essential states.

The main screen is only part of the work.

Within the agreed scope, we’ll identify the states needed to explain loading, missing information, validation, success, and failure. For products with multiple roles, we’ll clarify relevant differences in access and available actions.

### Make the journey reviewable.

A clickable Figma prototype lets you and your team follow the proposed sequence.

It supports discussion and can be used in separately scoped usability testing. A prototype demonstrates an interaction; it does not prove that the product has been validated.

### Prepare the work for development.

The handover will include the agreed design files and notes on behaviour, states, and reusable patterns.

Technical questions that affect the interface should be discussed with development before the design is treated as final.

## A collaborative process with clear decision points

**Define:** Agree on the audience, task, constraints, and evidence available.

**Structure:** Review the user flow and wireframes.

**Design:** Develop the interface and agreed states.

**Review:** Walk through the prototype and consolidate feedback.

**Hand over:** Deliver the agreed files and implementation context.

You’ll know when your input is needed and which decisions each review is intended to resolve.

## Questions about freelance UI/UX design

### Can we begin without formal user research?

Yes, but we should distinguish evidence from assumptions. We can use the information available and identify what needs later validation. Research can be scoped where access and timing allow.

### Can you design both web and mobile interfaces?

Yes. We’ll agree on the platform, screen sizes, and journeys before work begins. A second platform or substantially different layout is treated as additional scope.

### Do you work with an existing developer or team?

Yes. Early technical input helps surface constraints before they affect the final design. We can include agreed collaboration and handoff sessions.

### Will I receive an interactive prototype?

If included in the scope, yes. The prototype covers the agreed journey rather than every possible interaction in the application.

### Is UI/UX design the same as app development?

No. This service produces the agreed design deliverables. A production application requires implementation, infrastructure, testing, and deployment. Any development contribution is assessed and scoped separately.

### Can you improve our existing UI without redesigning everything?

Yes. We can focus on a particular flow, interface pattern, or set of states while respecting the rest of the product.

### Can you help after the handoff?

Implementation reviews or ongoing design support can be included in the proposal or arranged as a separate engagement.
